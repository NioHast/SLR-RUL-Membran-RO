#### ABSTRACT

The incorporation of Artificial Intelligence (AI) techniques into membrane-based systems has transformed their design, optimization, and management in various fields, notably, Reverse Osmosis (RO) and Ultrafiltration (UF). This comprehensive review evaluates the strategic application of AI methodologies across different membrane separation processes, furnishing practical insights for methodology selection and refinement. Ranging from predictive Machine Learning (ML) models for microfiltration to sophisticated neural networks for fouling alleviation in ultrafiltration, this review elucidates the alignment of AI approaches with specific application requisites. Despite persistent challenges such as data scarcity, interpretability of models, and computational demands, promising frontiers are emerging, including the amalgamation of AI with sensor technologies for real-time monitoring and control, and the utilization of generative adversarial networks for membrane material innovation. As the domain of AI in membrane separation progresses, interdisciplinary collaboration becomes paramount in surmounting existing obstacles and exploiting nascent prospects. This review underscores the necessity of comprehending AI methodologies tailored to diverse membrane separation context, steering future investigations towards heightened efficacy, sustainability, and ingenuity.

## **1. Introduction**

Membranes have been widely used since 1960s as selective barriers between phases [1–[3\]](#page-14-0), finding applications in various industries such as water and wastewater treatment [\[3\]](#page-14-0), desalination [\[4\]](#page-14-0), gas separation [[5](#page-14-0)], resource recovery [[6,7\]](#page-14-0), and food and beverage [\[8\]](#page-14-0). Membrane processes offer high separation capability, low energy consumption, and a small environmental footprint [[9](#page-14-0)]. Given the challenges of water scarcity and pollution [\[10](#page-14-0)], along with the need to reduce greenhouse gas emissions [[11\]](#page-14-0), membranes play a crucial role in ensuring the availability of fresh water, capturing and purifying carbon dioxide and methane, as well as recovering valuable resources and nutrients [\[12](#page-14-0)]. Additionally, membranes are increasingly important in the food and beverage sector, enabling improved filtration, emulsion, and separation capabilities for particles of different sizes [[13\]](#page-14-0).

In order to unlock the full potential of membrane processes, significant efforts in membrane fabrication and process parameters optimization have been carried out to increase separation capacity, and membrane lifetime and to reduce energy consumption [\[14\]](#page-14-0). A range of mechanistic mathematical and empirical methods have been implemented, leading to considerable progress in this field. Nevertheless, these methods could not yield sufficient satisfaction due to the

increasing complexity of systems and the need for more accurate modeling and predictions [[15\]](#page-14-0). As an alternative, in recent years, hybrid models, namely Artificial Intelligence (AI)-based hybrid models, have arisen from advances in both fields of phenomenological and empirical modeling. With this approach, a more complex and trustworthy model can be developed while reducing the computational load [[16\]](#page-14-0).

AI is focused on the development of smart systems which tackle problems in a comparable way to human intelligence systems [\[1\]](#page-14-0). AI is already vastly applied in different fields, such as smart cities, big data, and healthcare, focusing on performance prediction, system efficiency, and AI integration with the process [[14\]](#page-14-0). The revolutionary impact of AI has been so progressive that it has been referred to as the "4th paradigm of science" and the "4th industrial revolution" [[17\]](#page-14-0). Machine learning, as a subfield of artificial intelligence, has experienced fast progress in recent years. Statistical algorithms are at the core of machine-learning applications, whose performance improves with training [[18\]](#page-14-0). This type of approach is particularly useful when dealing with complex problems involving large combinatorial spaces, or nonlinear processes, which are either impossible to solve by traditional methods or can only be handled at a great computational cost. More recently, Deep Learning (DL) as a subclass of machine learning has gained huge attention. ### **2. Methodology**

#### *2.1. Data acquisition and screening*

The bibliographic data of membrane research from 1 January 2013 to 15 March 2023 were retrieved from the Web of Science database. The present study is limited to only research articles obtained from SCOPUS and Web of Science and screened following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) [[20\]](#page-14-0) stepby-step guidelines. First, 409 journal articles were obtained from the SCOPUS database with relevant keywords. The data selected for each publication includes title, author, affiliation, country, (authors-defined) keywords, publication year, and citation count. In the pre-screening process, review articles and articles without access to the full text were omitted. Of the remaining 313 articles, 109 of them were discarded due to not falling into the membrane filtration category.

#### *2.2. Data pre-processing*

Before performing the analysis, the retrieved data needed to be preprocessed to account for the keywords being mentioned by authors in different forms or with synonym terms. An example of this is Artificial Neural Networks (ANN). These terms and words are being combined to give an accurate counting of the frequencies they have appeared in research articles. Table S1, in the Supplementary Data, tabulated the list of similar keywords along with the replacement terms which is the most repeated keyword compared to its similar terms.

#### *3.3. Progress in artificial intelligence models and algorithms for membrane research*

Mechanistic modeling and non-mechanistic modeling are two distinct approaches used in studying complex systems, including membrane processes. Mechanistic models are based on fundamental physical, chemical, or biological principles and aim to explain system behavior using established laws and theories. These models provide insights into underlying mechanisms but can be limited by an incomplete understanding of physicochemical processes and may not generalize well to new conditions. In contrast, non-mechanistic or data-driven models focus on identifying patterns and relationships within data without relying on predetermined theories. These models are often more flexible

and better suited for handling large, complex datasets, but may lack explanatory power regarding underlying mechanisms. Conventionally, various membrane types and membrane processes have been studied using traditional mechanistic models. These models and modeling systems serve a function that cannot be replaced, but in some instances, our understanding of the physicochemical processes and interactions is not sufficient to develop an effective and accurate model [\[23](#page-14-0)]. Additionally, computational tools are essential when dealing with large and complex amounts of data resulting from monitoring membrane processes, allowing meaningful information to be extracted. The goal of mechanistic modeling is to explain the system and the experimental data in accordance with a priori understanding, while the goal of nonmechanistic modeling is to determine the system phenomena and dynamics that are inherent in the data. Moreover, the mechanistic models have a specific meaning that they have been developed based on data collected from a single experiment under certain operating conditions, which can mislead the model for new data. Complicated equation series formed because of the high number of variables in mechanistic models is another problem with these models which leads to complications of the model and a drastic increase in computation time [[24\]](#page-14-0).

AI-based technology has been propounded as an efficient tool for predicting membrane separation and filtration processes that can be used in the future, in order to overcome the limitations of traditional mechanistic models [[15\]](#page-14-0). Data-driven models generally outperform mechanistic models in prediction accuracy. For example, in a study by Dammau et al. [\[25](#page-14-0)] compared the performance of deterministic and data-driven models in describing fouling in MBRs as measured by the Trans Membrane Pressure (TMP) over 462 days. The data-driven model excelled in scenarios with significant fluctuations, such as low temperatures and varying pH or dissolved oxygen levels.

For generalization, which refers to how well a model performs on unseen or new data, data-driven models generally have better generalization ability, as they can capture complex relationships across varied datasets. For instance, in a study comparing mechanistic and ANNs for predicting tissue-to-plasma concentration ratios in rats, Nestorov et al. found that the ANN demonstrated superior generalizability and accuracy, with a mean prediction error of only 2 % compared to the mechanistic model's 18 % overprediction tendency [\[26](#page-14-0)].

In terms of computation time, data-driven models, once trained, can make predictions almost instantly, whereas mechanistic models often require significant computational resources due to the complexity of solving physical equations. In one case study, an LSTM neural network achieved a 30,000-fold computational acceleration compared to a mechanistic PDE model for simulating a synthetic gene circuit [\[27](#page-14-0)].

Due to the wide variety of methods and their capabilities within artificial intelligence, different classifications have been suggested. However, the most common way is to categorize these algorithms based on the type of data used for modeling in three broad classes as suggested in [Fig. 2a](#page-4-0): supervised learning, unsupervised learning, and reinforcement learning [\[28](#page-14-0)]. Specifically, supervised learning is a machine learning technique that involves training a model on labeled data, which means that each input is associated with a corresponding output. The goal of supervised learning is to learn a route from input to output that can be used to make predictions on unseen data. Common examples of supervised learning include image classification, speech recognition, and natural language processing [\[29](#page-14-0)]. One of the most popular supervised learning algorithms is the decision tree algorithm. Decision Trees (DT) are a visual representation of the decision-making process, where each node represents a decision, and each branch represents a possible outcome. DTs are used for both classification and regression tasks, making them versatile algorithms [\[30](#page-14-0)]. The k-means clustering algorithm is one of the most popular unsupervised learning algorithms. Kmeans clustering is a technique used to partition a dataset into k groups based on similarities between data points. The data points are iteratively assigned by the algorithm to a cluster and the cluster centroid is updated until convergence. K-means clustering is widely used in various

#### *3.3.1. Artificial neural network*

An effective and currently popular approach for anticipating and simulating a procedure is through the use of an artificial neural network. ANN is designed to mimic the functionality of the human brain, which involves neurons and weights that are utilized for modeling and predicting various parameters. Additionally, the neurons in an ANN communicate with one another through weights [[37\]](#page-14-0).

This type of neural network operates like a black box model, meaning that it is not constrained by physical laws and its parameters may not have any physical significance. The primary aim of ANN is to establish a relationship between independent input variables and dependent output variables [[38\]](#page-14-0). This relationship is derived through a learning process in which the network is fed a dataset, making it a data-driven model. ANN

has become increasingly popular due to its ability to quickly and accurately map non-linear relationships between system inputs and outputs, resulting in decreased computational time [\[39](#page-14-0)].

The training of an ANN typically involves one of three main learning paradigms: supervised, unsupervised, or hybrid. Multilayer perception (MLP) is one of the most powerful methods of ANN [\[40](#page-14-0)]. An MLP, or multi-layer perceptron, is composed of different layers including an input layer, at least one hidden layer, and an output layer as shown in Fig. 3a. The number of neurons or nodes in each layer is determined to match the number of input and output parameters. Depending on the optimal architecture of the ANN model, the hidden layers may have several neurons [\[39\]](#page-14-0). During the training phase, the weight of each neuron in the hidden and output layer is computed and assigned. Additionally, a bias or threshold value is included as a weighted unit input for each neuron in the hidden and output layer.

Several MLP-ANN algorithms are developed which Back Propagation Feedforward Neural Network (BPFFNN) is among the most used ones including Radial Basis Function Neural Network (RBFNN) [\[41](#page-14-0)], and Deep Neural Network (DNN) [[42\]](#page-14-0), Elman Neural Network (ENN), Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) [[43\]](#page-14-0).

#### 3.3.3. Random forest

Random forest is a model that uses trees and combines the strengths of tree-based models while addressing the limitations of traditional classification or regression trees. Decision tree models are particularly susceptible to overfitting, even with proper hyper-parameter tuning. This means that the decision tree model may not perform well in predicting outcomes on new, unseen data [49]. Random forest, on the other hand, overcomes the overfitting issue by utilizing randomness in selecting bootstrapped datasets and constructing trees based on a random subset of features [50]. This has made the RF model a popular and dependable machine-learning model for predicting the performance of various membrane systems as shown in Fig. 3c.

To train the RF model, there are three primary stages involved: First, the main training dataset is used to create multiple subsets called "bootstrapped datasets". The number of subsets formed is equal to the number of trees in the forest. It is important to note that the size of each bootstrapped dataset is the same as the training dataset, meaning that the same sample can be selected multiple times. Then, each bootstrapped dataset is utilized to generate a tree and a random subset of features in order to construct a tree. Finally, the process in step 2 is repeated to create a new tree with a different bootstrapped dataset and randomly selected features until all the trees in the forest have been formed [51].

As shown in Fig. 3c, random forest uses majority voting or averaging of the predictions of the independent trees to produce an output; reducing the variance and improving the generalization of the model due to the high number of estimators used [52].

#### 3.3.4. Gradient boosting

Gradient boosting methods are a subset of ensemble methods similar to bagging methods (i.e., DT and RF). However, despite the bagging methods, in boosting, the accuracy of prediction is improved through serial learning [53]. Adaptive Boosting (AdaBoost), Categorical

Boosting (CatBoost), Gradient Boosting (GB), and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) are some of the popular boosting methods used by data scientists and engineers.

In boosting, models are built sequentially by minimizing the errors from previous models while increasing (boosting) the influence of high-performing models [54]. In order to minimize the errors in sequential models, a gradient descent algorithm is added to form the gradient boosting approach. Finally, GB through parallel processing, tree pruning, handling missing values and regularization to avoid overfitting/bias yields XGBoost which is capable of producing superior results using fewer computing resources in the shortest amount of time [55].

The selection of an appropriate AI approach for membrane studies depends on the criteria that are related to the data, type of membrane, and application context. Basic methods, such as decision trees or random forests, should be prioritized for structured or moderately complex data. Sophisticated models, such as neural networks or deep learning, are necessary for more intricate problems. The type of membrane should also be taken into account; for example, polymeric membranes may only require ensemble learning techniques, whereas more complex systems, such as nanocomposite or MMM, typically necessitate advanced models that can encapsulate intricate material interactions. Additionally, the context of the application should be considered. For example, evolutionary algorithms or genetic optimization are advantageous for optimization tasks, such as membrane design, whereas reinforcement learning or time-series models like LSTM are the most effective approaches for real-time monitoring and control, including contamination detection. Hybrid methodologies, which incorporate a variety of AI techniques, are frequently employed to enhance the accuracy and interpretability of forecasts, thereby allowing models to accommodate both the inherent physical limitations and the complexity of the system. It is beneficial to employ and compare multiple proven methods like ANN, RF, SVM, and XGBoost, assessing their performance using accuracy indicators such as R<sup>2</sup>, RMSE, MSE, and MAE. These metrics are vital in evaluating AI algorithms in regression tasks.

#### *4.1. AI and microfiltration*

Microfiltration (MF) membranes are the oldest of the four pressuredriven membrane processes [\[127](#page-16-0)] with separation pressures ranging from 100 to 400 kPa [[128](#page-16-0)], which are the lowest pressure requirements, and highest permeability when compared to the other pressure driven membranes. The membrane separation layer works like a filter, allowing only particles smaller than 100–10,000 nm and molecules with molecular weights *<*100–500 Da to pass through while blocking larger particles and heavier molecules. [\[15](#page-14-0)]. Polymeric materials are most used for MF membranes, however, a wide range of materials are available for application on this membrane type including ceramics where SiO2 and Al2O3 are the most commonly used non-organic MF membrane materials [[129\]](#page-16-0).

As shown in Fig. 5a, MF membranes have been spotted in the literature only 14 times which is one of the lowest investigated membrane module types by AI. This can be related to the recent nature of the application of AI in membrane studies as opposed to the long history of MF membranes in the chemical and material engineering field leading to the overlooking of researchers to this particular module. Liu et al. [[130](#page-16-0)] developed an ANN model for the turbulence promoter-assisted Crossflow Microfiltration (CFMF) process using experimental data, in which the inlet velocity, TMP, and feed concentration were taken as inputs and the Flux Improvement Efficiency (FIE) by turbulence promoter as output with a prediction accuracy estimated by R-value of 0.85. According to the result of the sensitivity analysis based on the correlation coefficient, the filtration time was the most significant input, followed by temperature, cross-flow velocity, feed pH, and TMP. A more recent study by Tanudjaja et al. [[131](#page-16-0)] aimed to improve the prediction of critical flux utilizing machine learning models, and employed RF and NN algorithms on a dataset of 223 data sets from various studies, including nine operational parameters. The results showed that crossflow velocity was the most dominant parameter, followed by foulant size and charge, in determining critical flux via the RF model. Additionally, the NN models provided accurate predictions of critical flux with an R2 consistently above 0.9. Unlike the previous studies focusing mostly on water/ wastewater treatment application of MF membrane, which as shown is

the most investigated subject area of AI-assisted membrane studies, Abdullah et al. [\[132\]](#page-16-0) have implemented the ANN-GA method for tannin reduction and clarification of cashew apple juice using pressure-driven CFMF. ANN has successfully determined the optimum process condition for MF in terms of membrane pore size and TMP in order to maximize the tannin content, turbidity, and color. The study also developed a resistance-in-series model that accurately represented the flux behavior of the microfiltration system.

#### *4.2. AI and ultrafiltration*

Ultrafiltration (UF) membranes operate using the same separation principles as MF membranes, with UF driving pressures varying from 100 to 1000 kPa and their pore size ranging from 0.001 to 1 μm [[133](#page-16-0)]. The main difference between the two membrane technologies is their ability to reject smaller molecules like macromolecules, colloids, and solutes with molecular weight larger than 10,000 Da [\[128\]](#page-16-0). The simplicity, overall low cost of the process, low energy usage, fewer control techniques, little or less emphasis on process chemicals, mild operating temperature, and high-quality treatment of the UF process are the main reasons why they have replaced conventional purification and disinfection processes [[134](#page-16-0)].

In an effort to thoroughly analyze UF membranes performance and fabrication conditions, four different supervised learning (SL) approaches - multiple additive regression trees (MART), NN, LR, and SVM – have been utilized by Chi et al. [[90](#page-15-0)]. Based on the analysis, the most reliable and trustworthy SL model was identified to be NN with R2 values of 0.8992 and 0.9559 for the rejection rate and the flux prediction. This model was then used to optimize the fabrication conditions and predict the optimal performance of PVC/PVB ultrafiltration membranes. As can be perceived from [Fig.](#page-10-0) *6*, membrane design and fabrication is the most investigated objective of the study by membrane scientists using AI techniques after process optimization.

[\[71](#page-15-0)] to assess the effectiveness of a FO–UF hybrid system by measuring water flux and RhB removal. To achieve this, the researchers employed RSM and Back Propagation Artificial Neural Network (BPANN) models to forecast and optimize the experimental outcomes using various operational parameters (initial RhB

#### *4.3. AI and nanofiltration*

Nanofiltration (NF) membranes are another pressure-driven membrane technology with high rejection efficiencies with most dissolved organic solutes and good salt rejection at salt concentrations varying from 1000 to 2000 ppm [[135](#page-16-0)]. Typical NF pore size ranges from 0.001 to 0.01 μm and MWCO value of 150–2000 Da [[136\]](#page-16-0). The rejection mechanism of nanofiltration membranes is not only based on size exclusion, as seen with other membrane types, but it is a combination of size exclusion, charge repulsion and adsorption [\[137,138](#page-16-0)].

An increasing interest can be noticed in the study of organic solvent nanofiltration (OSN) processes with AI in recent years [[63,](#page-15-0)[139,140](#page-16-0)]. A novel quantitative structure–activity relationship (QSAR) is proposed by Ignacz and Szekely [[141](#page-16-0)] for precise prediction of solute rejection in OSN membranes. An extensive database is used for the training of two algorithms of partial least-squares (PLS) regression and message-passing neural networks (MPNN). In the database, molecule structures are stored as SMILES (Simplified Molecular-Input Line-Entry System) which is essentially a direct string representation of a molecule. In this way, a model is able to investigate dipper layers of structural behaviors of membrane, pollutants and solutions used in the process and link the observations with underlying phenomena such as functional groups, and molecular weight. The applied methodology in this article, along with a Graphical User Interface (GUI) rejection prediction tool available at [www.osndatabase.com](http://www.osndatabase.com) for free usage by researchers, boosts the application of OSN in novel membrane design and different industrial sectors. In another study on NF, Zhang et al. [\[18](#page-14-0)] have addressed the data scarcity problem of DL models which generally leads to overfitting, by extracting the natural features of monomer molecular structures and then distorting them in a reasonable manner. The rejection and flux predictions of polyamide NF membranes are carried out by molecular augmentation on DNN with *<*5 % mean relative error.

Baig et al. [[142](#page-16-0)] have explored the application of machine learning

techniques to predict the performance of loose nanofiltration membranes in dye and salt removal from wastewater. Using experimental data from a laboratory setup, four computational models were evaluated: hybrid adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system (ANFIS), robustlinear regression (RLR), support vector regression (SVR), and multilinear regression (MLR). The results showed that SVR-M2 performed best for predicting flux and rejection, while ANFIS-M2 excelled in modeling dye/salt rejection. This research demonstrates the potential of combining experimental membrane design with machine learning to optimize the fractionation of divalent salts and dyes in wastewater treatment.

## *4.3.1. AI and reverse osmosis*

Reverse osmosis (RO) membranes are pressure-driven semipermeable membranes with a separation range between 0.1 and 1 nm, used for the removal of solutes such as small molecular weight molecules (e.g., organic compounds) and salts, while allowing water molecules to permeate through the membrane [\[39\]](#page-14-0). RO membrane's pore size range is so small that it results in slower permeability, thus requiring the application of high pressures to overcome this limitation [\[143\]](#page-16-0). RO membranes can reach the rejection of up to 99.5 % of small particles, making it an appropriate technology for applications requiring high rejection efficiencies such as desalination and treatment, and reuse of wastewater [[144](#page-16-0)].

Operational cost optimization is another important objective that has been at the center of attention for membrane technologies that have found their way into the industry but still need considerable investments compared to conventional technologies [\[145\]](#page-16-0). An example of such an endeavor is the work of Nazif et al. [\[146\]](#page-16-0) in which NN-based optimization of the RO system is practiced in order to reduce fouling, increase membrane life span, and minimize system costs. According to the model's results, controlling inlet pressure, inflow, and system recovery can prolong the membrane's life for small RO systems and minimize cleaning cycles. In another similar study, Srivastava et al. [[147](#page-16-0)] used ANN for early fault prediction and predictive maintenance of RO plants. Five plant parameters namely, pH and TDS of product water, feed and product water flow rates and washing cycles along with plant operation time are used as inputs to predict membrane efficiency with a very high accuracy of R2 = 99.99 %. However, the results must be approached with caution, since the rather small size of the database, i.e., 75 samples

accompanied by exceptionally high accuracy, suggests the possibility of overfitting.

## *4.4. AI and forward osmosis*

FO membranes are based on osmotic processes that require the use of a higher concentration draw solution (DS) compared to the lower concentration Feed Solution (FS) [[148,149\]](#page-16-0). The high osmotic pressure gradient between the DS and FS drives the water transport mechanism and the net flow of water molecules across the semipermeable membrane [[150](#page-16-0)]. The diluted DS can either be recovered and separated from the water, with an additional separation step, or it can be used as is [[151](#page-16-0)]. In comparison to RO membrane processes, FO does not require any additional significant energy for its operation, however in the case of DS regeneration a significant energy input is needed [[152](#page-16-0)].

Up to now, some critical issues have hindered the widespread industrialization of FO membranes such as reversal solute flux (RSF) and concentration polarization (CP) [[153](#page-16-0)]. Ibrar et al. [[111](#page-16-0)] investigated the ability of several AI algorithms such as ANN, XGBoost, CatBoost, RF and LR in accurate prediction of internal CP with superior performance observed for CatBoost in terms of RSME and R2 (0.92). This study proves the applicability of AI methods in prediction of very sophisticated physical phenomena laid underneath FO process, especially considering the appropriate feature selection carried out by researchers, ruling out the most correlated parameters (*>*0.95) in order to obtain a more robust model. On the other hand, there is a challenge to current wastewater treatment technologies due to the presence of trace amount of contaminants known as micropollutants [\[154\]](#page-16-0). The process of 97 MPs removal by FO membrane was recently studied by Viet and Jang [[155](#page-16-0)]. It has been discovered that FO has a very low rejection capability for MPs with low molecular weight (2–80 %) with a noticeably precise rejection prediction from the modifier ANFIS method (R2 = 0.99 and RMSE = 0.56 %).

## *4.5. AI and membrane bioreactor*

Membrane bioreactors (MBRs) are a type of wastewater treatment system that combines both biological treatment processes with membrane filtration and separation technologies [[156,157\]](#page-16-0). A MBR's treatment effectiveness is higher than that of other biological systems due to the extremely dense microbial population that surrounds the membrane surface and ensures that all pollutants are eliminated before the wastewater passes through the membrane [158–[160\]](#page-16-0). In addition, because of the sieving effect of the membranes, pollutants with molecular weights higher than the molecular weight cut-off of the membranes are maintained, bringing them into touch with the microorganisms responsible for their destruction inside the MBR [\[161](#page-17-0)].

As mentioned, fouling is a critical issue in membrane studies, as such to emphasize the importance of fouling studies in membranes, it was decided to present the fouling reduction as a separate category in [Fig. 6](#page-10-0). Fouling plays an even more critical role in the case of membrane bioreactors. Thus, Woo et al. [\[162\]](#page-17-0) introduce a new technique for estimating the lifespan of a membrane and reducing the expenses associated with membrane maintenance based on monitoring the functional profile of the membrane using functional machine learning. 12 functional parameters related to biological, chemical, and physical properties derived from TMP and chemical dosage are implemented to identify membrane ageing and lifespan through hidden patterns and trends in these functional parameters in a full-scale MBR plant. The findings indicate that the proposed technique improved the efficiency and accuracy of membrane replacement time detection. In another work by Taheri et al. [\[74](#page-15-0)], ANFIS and ANN algorithms are implemented to predict TMP in an anaerobic membrane bioreactor-sequencing batch reactor (AnMBR-SBR) during biohydrogen production. The study used organic loading rates, effluent pH, mixed liquor suspended solids, and mixed liquor volatile suspended solids as input parameters. The ANFIS model was

trained using hybrid algorithms for TMP prediction, and the Gauss membership function with four membership numbers provided the highest prediction performance. The findings of this study suggest that AI techniques can be a valuable tool in predicting operational parameters in wastewater treatment, during biohydrogen production with R2 values of at least 0.88.

#### *4.6. AI and membrane distillation*

Membrane distillation is a membrane-based separation process that has emerged as a promising technology with broad applications [[163](#page-17-0)]. This process entails the selective vaporization of a liquid across a hydrophobic membrane, driven by a vapor pressure gradient [[164](#page-17-0)]. Unlike conventional separation methods, membrane distillation operates at low temperatures, making it advantageous for separating temperaturesensitive substances. It offers numerous benefits, including high selectivity, energy efficiency, and the ability to handle complex mixtures [[165](#page-17-0)]. Membrane distillation has demonstrated potential in diverse fields such as desalination, wastewater treatment, food processing, and chemical purification [[166](#page-17-0)].

Due to the special characteristics of this membrane module, accurate modeling and estimation of heat transfer is a requirement. Dudchen et al. [[91\]](#page-15-0) have presented a method for assessing the accuracy of empirical methods used to approximate membrane permeability and heat transfer rate in MD. They have Neural Networks on process and membrane parameters to improve accuracy, demonstrating that the inclusion of membrane thermal conductivity, pore size, and porosity variables in an empirical MD model substantially improves estimation accuracy. The results suggest that this method can be applied to other membrane separation processes as well. Later, in a hybrid membrane system study, Qamar et al. [\[42](#page-14-0)] tested the applicability and prediction accuracy of a NN trained with a UF membrane on membrane distillation. The results showed that the NN was able to accurately estimate biofilm thickness on both types of membranes using OCT images, demonstrating its potential for use in a variety of filtration systems. This technology has the potential to improve water treatment processes by providing realtime monitoring of biofilm growth and its impact on hydrodynamics parameters.

#### **5. Unlocking membrane research potential with AI: Challenges and emerging areas**

Up to this point, it has been elaborated that the integration of artificial intelligence into membrane research has the potential to revolutionize the development and optimization of membrane filtration processes for several applications. Process optimization, membrane design and fabrication along with fouling reduction and process control will keep the spotlight on them for future research as the main objectives of implementing AI techniques in membrane research; however, in order to address these matters in a more comprehensive manner it is highly advised to pay attention into the development of extensive, reliable and standard datasets available to all researchers since it can be perceived from [Fig. 7](#page-12-0)a that a very wide range of parameters is being used as input for developing AI models. For instance, for studies aimed at membrane design and fabrication, membrane characteristics are essential to be considered fully in the dataset whereas, for studies targeting process optimization, operating conditions would suffice. #### **6. Conclusion**

In conclusion, this bibliometric review provides valuable insights into the application of artificial intelligence techniques in membrane technologies for separation purposes and its evolvement during the past decade. The integration of AI has significant potential to enhance the performance and sustainability of membrane separation processes for various applications. It is highlighted that AI techniques have been applied successfully in the modeling, optimization, and control of membrane separation processes. Machine learning algorithms such as NN, SVM, DT, and RF have been used to develop predictive models for various types of membranes such as RO, NF, UF, and MF. These models can predict permeate flux, rejection rate, fouling rate, and other performance parameters with high accuracy. Moreover, AI-based optimization algorithms such as GA, PSO, and Bayesian optimization have been used to optimize operating conditions such as pressure, temperature, and flow rate to maximize performance while minimizing energy consumption and fouling. AI-based control strategies such as model predictive control also have been used to maintain optimal operating conditions in real-time and to act as an early fault detection system.

Challenges associated with the integration of AI into membrane research such as data availability issues, model interpretability issues, and computational complexity can be overcome by collaborative and connected research works allowing the growth of reliable databases for different membrane applications, also by developing hybrid models that combine physics-based models with data-driven models (e.g., ANFIS & ANN). Prospects include further development of hybrid models that combine physics-based models with data-driven models to improve model accuracy and interpretability. Moreover, developing explainable AI techniques will enable researchers to understand how the model makes predictions and decisions. This will increase trust in the model's predictions and facilitate its adoption in the industry. AI-assisted membrane studies are promising in emerging fields, including micropollutant removal, FO membranes, membranes in the food and beverage industry, circular economy, and energy management.
