#### ABSTRACT

Optimizing membrane performance for efficient water treatment is crucial for sustainable development and environmental protection, aligning with UN SDGs. This study involves experimental design, statistical reliability of small data, and explainable machine learning (ML) using SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations). The research uses ML models and statistical tests to ensure data reliability and stationarity and investigate various membranes' fouling and separation efficiency (MX-CM, PDMX-CM, and SPDMX-CM). Stationarity tests, including the Augmented Dickey–Fuller (ADF) and Phillips–Perron (PP) tests, revealed that MX-CM is stationary at level (I(0)), while PDMX-CM and SPDMX-CM required first differencing (I(1)) to achieve stationarity. SHAP analysis showed that in the fouling study, higher values of PDMX-CM and MX-CM positively influenced model predictions, with SHAP values of +0.09 for Cycle, -0.06 for PDMX-CM, and -0.06 for MX-CM. In the separation efficiency study, Cycle had a neutral impact (0.00), PDMX-CM had a slight positive effect, and MX-CM had a slight negative impact. These findings highlight the importance of ensuring data stationarity and utilizing SHAP for model explainability in predicting membrane performance. Accurate preprocessing and model interpretation enhance decision-making and optimization in membrane fouling and separation efficiency studies, ensuring robust and reliable ML models.

## **2. Methods and methodology**

# *2.1. Synthesis of the sulofnated the MXene*

The MXene sheets were synthesized by etching the MAX phase with the HF. The modification of the MXene with the sulfonated groups consists of several steps. For delamination of the MXene sheets and to get the uniform dispersion of its sheets, the MXene was sonicated for 2 h. After delimitation of the MXene sheets, the dopamine was added into the MXene solution, and the pH was increased to 8.5, which resulted in the initiation of the polymerization of the dopamine. The solution prepared for membrane fabrication was consist of dopamine (0.5 mg/ML) and MXene (1 mg/ML). The reaction mixture was kept at 60 ◦C for 24 h to enhance the polymerization. The pristine MXene solution was black in color, and it turned to dark brownish black. For sulfonation, the 2,5-diamino benzenesulfonic acid was added to the MXene and dopamine solution during the polymerization of the dopamine. This results in the formation of sulfonated MXene. The 2,5-diamino benzenesulfonic acid concentration of 0.1 mg/ML were used to react with the polydopamine. Further, details of the sulfonation of the MXene have already been discussed in the previously published literature [\(Baig et al., 2023c\)](#page-12-0).

## <span id="page-2-0"></span>*2.2. Designing sulfonated MXene membrane*

The sulfonated MXene separating layer was prepared on the surface of the ceramic membrane. A 1 mg/ML solution of the MXene was prepared in double distilled water. To form the separating layer, 25 ML was taken from the stock solution, diluted 10 times, and subjected to sonication to separate the sheets further. The dead-end filtration assembly was used to form the separating layer. Therefore, the ceramic membrane was installed into the separation assembly. The sulfonated polydopamine functionalized MXene was added into the dead-end cell and slowly passed at a pressure of 0.5 bar, where sulfonated polydopamine functionalized MXene kept self-assembling to form an MXene-based separating layer on the surface of the ceramic-membrane. After forming the barrier layer, it was compacted at a 1 and 2 bar pressure, respectively. MX-CM represents the membranes which only have the pristine MXene as separating layer on the surface of the ceramic membrane whereas the PDMX-CM membranes have s separating layer of the polydopamine functionalized MXene sheets. The SPDMX-CM represents the membranes in which the separating layer is formed by using the Sulfonated polydopamine functionalized MXene [\(Anjum et al., 2022](#page-11-0)). The surface morphology of the SPDMX-CM membrane can be seen in Fig. S1.

## *2.3. Preparation of oil-in-water emulsions*

The surfactant-stabilized oil-in-water emulsion was prepared with the help of the tween-80. In brief, 0.2 g of diesel oil was added to 1 L of water, which contained 0.5 g of the surfactant. The stirring was done at 800 rpm for 24 hours. After that, the milky colour oil-in-water emulsion was formed.

## *2.4. Sulfonated polydopamine functionalized MXene membrane performance evaluation*

The sulfonated polydopamine-functionalized MXene membrane was installed into the dead-end cell to assess its evaluation and fouling resistance. The membranes' permeation flux was measured using Eq. (1).

## **3. Proposed data-driven methods**

## *3.1. Explainability AI (SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP))*

ML models often achieve exceptional performance in predictive tasks but tend to lack interpretability, rendering them "black boxes". Understanding the complex nonlinear interactions between features within these models is crucial. SHAP address this issue by providing a means to interpret and understand model behavior and the relationships between features. SHAP values, derived from game theory concepts introduced by Lundberg and Lee, quantify the contribution of each feature to the model's predictions ([Lu et al., 2023](#page-12-0); [Anjum et al., 2022\)](#page-11-0). These values can indicate both positive and negative impacts on the output. This study employs various SHAP plots, including feature importance, summary plots, force plots, decision plots, and feature dependence plots, to elucidate the model's inner workings ([Lundberg and Lee, 2017\)](#page-12-0). By leveraging SHAP values, researchers can assess the significance of feature contributions, enhancing the transparency and interpretability of complex ML models ([Lehnert et al., 2024\)](#page-12-0).

Explainable ML is crucial for understanding and improving the reliability analysis of separation efficiency and anti-fouling performance in sulfonated polydopamine functionalized MXene-based membranes within surfactant-stabilized oil-in-water emulsions [\(Arrieta et al., 2024](#page-11-0)). By enhancing interpretability, transparency, error diagnosis, and regulatory compliance, explainable ML enables researchers to distinguish how variables such as membrane properties, surfactant concentration, and emulsion characteristics impact performance outcomes. Techniques like feature importance ranking, partial dependence plots, SHAP values, and LIME facilitate this interpretability (Rodríguez-P´[erez and Bajorath,](#page-12-0)  [2020;](#page-12-0) [Yan et al., 2020\)](#page-12-0). These methods explain the decision-making processes of complex models, building trust and aiding in error rectification. In this context, data collection and preprocessing, model building, and the application of explainability techniques are critical steps. Through these processes, explainable ML bridges the gap between high-performance predictive models and the need for transparent, reliable insights in scientific research and industrial applications, ultimately enhancing the trustworthiness and robustness of ML models used in the analysis of advanced materials and processes ([Iban, 2022](#page-12-0)).

The function of the model, g(x') is defined as Eq. (4)

$$g(x') = \phi_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{M} \phi_i x' \tag{4}$$

#### *3.2. Concept of data reliability, normality and stationarity*

Data reliability is crucial for ensuring the consistency and accuracy of data, which underpins the validity and dependability of results in modeling processes. Ensuring data reliability, normality, and stationarity enhances model performance, interpretability, and generalizability, which is essential for accurate predictions and robust conclusions in various fields, including chemistry, material science, environmental science, and material studies. To ensure data reliability and assess stationarity in datasets, various statistical methods are employed. For data reliability, methods like Cronbach's Alpha measure internal consistency, Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC) assesses measurement reliability, Bland-Altman Analysis evaluates agreement between different measurement techniques, and Kuder-Richardson Formula 20 (KR-20) is used for dichotomous items [\(Hamisu et al.,](#page-12-0)  [2017\)](#page-12-0). Normality tests such as the Shapiro-Wilk Test, Kolmogorov-Smirnov Test, Anderson-Darling Test, and Lilliefors Test verify if data follows a normal distribution [\(Mati, 2021\)](#page-12-0). Stationarity tests like the ADF Test, Phillips-Perron (PP) Test, Kwiatkowski-Phillips-Schmidt-Shin (KPSS) Test, and Zivot-Andrews Test check for unit roots and structural breaks in time series data. Homogeneity of variance tests, including Levene's Test, Bartlett's Test, and Brown-Forsythe Test, assess the equality of variances across groups. Additionally, test-retest reliability and split-half reliability measure stability and consistency over time. Robustness and outlier detection methods like Mahalanobis Distance and Cook's Distance identify influential data points. These comprehensive statistical methods are critical in ensuring the accuracy and reliability of predictive models, particularly in fields like environmental science where data quality directly impacts the validity of conclusions [\(Shuabiu et al., 2021\)](#page-12-0). In ML and statistical modelling, reliable data significantly affects the performance, interpretability, and generalizability of models [\(Yu et al., 2022](#page-12-0)).

Further numerical analysis was conducted using correlograms, which include the Autocorrelation Function (ACF) and Partial Autocorrelation Function (PACF), which is essential in assessing data reliability and enhancing modeling in time series analysis. The ACF measures the correlation between observations at different lags, helping to identify patterns, seasonality, and trends, while the PACF measures the direct correlation after accounting for earlier lags, assisting in the specification of autoregressive models [\(Wilson, 2016](#page-12-0)). These functions are crucial for determining the appropriate parameters for ARIMA models and ensuring correct model specification. Additionally, ACF and PACF plots help detect stationarity, a key assumption in time series modeling, indicating whether data transformations like differencing are needed. After fitting a model, examining the ACF and PACF of residuals helps diagnose model adequacy, ensuring that residuals resemble white noise. This diagnostic step is vital for refining models to improve accuracy. By identifying and addressing issues such as seasonality, trends, and correlations through ACF and PACF, the quality of data preprocessing is enhanced, leading to more reliable and accurate predictive models [\(Wilson, 2016\)](#page-12-0).

## *3.3. Small data handling*

The ML algorithms, known for the data-driven black-box approach, have been successfully used to map trends in chemistry and materials science without needing prior knowledge of underlying physical mechanisms. However, a significant challenge in material science is the

acquisition of small datasets due to the high complexity of experimental procedures ([Frey et al., 2019b](#page-12-0)). Researchers often prefer to collect smaller, high-quality samples in controlled environments rather than relying on larger datasets with uncertain origins. This preference highlights the need to distinguish between "big data" and "small data" within the fields of computer science and material science. Xu et al. [\(2023](#page-12-0)). noted that most datasets used in material science ML are categorized as small data [\(Xu et al., 2023](#page-12-0)). Despite this, the predictive goals of ML models remain consistent, requiring careful handling of small datasets due to increased uncertainty compared to larger datasets. Challenges such as underfitting, overfitting, and data imbalance are more pronounced with small datasets, making accurate data preprocessing and post-processing essential. Selecting appropriate modeling algorithms is also critical to ensure the reliability and accuracy of predictions. By prioritizing these steps, researchers can mitigate the inherent challenges of working with small data in material science ML, thereby enhancing the effectiveness of their models [\(Liang and Zhu, 2019\)](#page-12-0).

The "Small data" refers to datasets that are small in size but rich in information content, focusing on quality and depth rather than volume. Small data is powerful in modeling due to its ability to provide focused insights, its manageability, and its efficiency, requiring less computational power ([Fawcett, 2019\)](#page-12-0). Leveraging small data involves maximizing the value of limited datasets through careful data preprocessing, feature engineering, and applying robust statistical and ML methods designed for small samples. The objective is to extract meaningful patterns and make reliable predictions despite the smaller dataset size. This approach allows for precise, actionable insights, reduced risk of errors and biases, and efficient model performance ([Jahani et al.,](#page-12-0)  [2023\)](#page-12-0). Fig. 2a outlines the workflows and the general process of experimental and modern-based ML-assisted materials design. As illustrated in Fig. 2b, the approaches to handling small data are discussed at three levels. At the data source level, strategies include data extraction from publications, constructing materials databases, and employing high-throughput computations and experiments. At the algorithm level, methods focus on modelling algorithms suitable for small data and techniques for imbalanced learning.

## **5. Conclusion**

This study evaluated the fouling and separation efficiency of MX-CM, PDMX-CM, and SPDMX-CM membranes using a combination of statistical tests and machine learning (ML) models. The results demonstrated that ensuring data stationarity is critical for reliable ML modeling. MX-CM consistently showed stable performance data, making it a reliable candidate for further analysis, while PDMX-CM and SPDMX-CM required preprocessing steps like differencing to achieve stationarity. SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) analysis provided valuable insights into the influence of different features on model predictions. In the fouling study, higher values of PDMX-CM and MX-CM positively impacted predictions, with Cycle having a moderate positive effect. In contrast, in the separation efficiency study, Cycle had a neutral impact, while PDMX-CM and MX-CM showed slight differential effects. The study underscores the importance of rigorous data preprocessing and the use of explainable ML techniques to optimize membrane performance in water treatment. These findings are crucial for advancing membrane technology, supporting environmental sustainability, and aligning with global standards like the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) guidelines. The key findings are summarized as follow:

- Ensuring data stationarity is crucial for reliable ML modeling. MX-CM shows stable performance data and requires no difference, while PDMX-CM and SPDMX-CM need preprocessing.
- MX-CM membrane demonstrated consistent stability, making it a reliable option for further analysis, while PDMX-CM and SPDMX-CM required additional steps to achieve similar reliability.

- SHAP analysis revealed that higher values of PDMX-CM and MX-CM positively influenced fouling predictions, with Cycle having a moderate positive impact.
- In the separation efficiency study, SHAP analysis indicated that Cycle had a neutral impact, while PDMX-CM and MX-CM showed slight but differential effects on model predictions.
- Accurate data preprocessing, including differencing, is necessary to ensure the robustness of time-series models used in membrane performance studies.
- The findings support optimizing membrane performance in water treatment, contributing to environmental sustainability and aligning with global standards like the SDGs and EPA guidelines.
- Based on our findings, future research could explore the development of advanced pre-processing techniques to further enhance the stationarity and reliability of membrane performance data, particularly for PDMX-CM and SPDMX-CM membranes.
- Moreover, future studies could investigate the application of SHAP analysis in real-time monitoring and optimization of membrane processes, aiming to improve predictive accuracy and operational efficiency in industrial water treatment settings.
